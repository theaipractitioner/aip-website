If you manage a team that has not started on AI, a new survey from Meta will make you feel late. It says 84% of small business owners across Asia Pacific already use it.

Read the whole release before you feel anything, though. The headline number is the weakest part of it. The strongest part is about what those owners say they need next.

## What the survey found

Meta commissioned the research firm YouGov to survey almost 4,000 small business owners in nine markets: Australia, Singapore, Vietnam, Indonesia, Taiwan, India, Thailand, Japan and Hong Kong. Meta published the results on 14 October 2026.

On Meta's figures, 84% of the owners currently use AI. Looking back over the past 12 months, 76% report a positive effect on productivity and 71% report a positive effect on revenue or sales. 80% agree that AI helps small businesses compete with larger ones.

Then comes the figure that matters most. 86% say small businesses need more guidance to get the full benefit. 51% name cost as the leading barrier to training.

## What they use it for

The uses are ordinary. Among owners whose business used AI in the past year, 51% create or edit content and 43% do local advertising and marketing. Another 38% use it for translation and 37% for customer support and communications.

None of that needs a technical team. It is writing, selling, translating and replying to customers, the same tasks a department head already has on the list.

The owners who report a productivity gain say where the saved time goes. 58% use it to improve the quality of work they already do. 50% put it into growth and planning, and 46% get more done with the same team.

Notice the first answer. More than half of the owners who report a gain are not cutting hours. They are using the time to make existing work better.

## Why the 84% needs a caveat

Meta sells advertising and business tools to small businesses. In the same release it promotes its own Small Business Growth Academy. A company with that interest has good reason to find that AI helps small firms, and to find that they want training.

That does not make the figures false. It does mean you should read them as Meta's, not as an independent measure of adoption.

Several limits apply. All of the effects are reported by the owners themselves, so nobody measured a productivity gain. The release does not say how it defines a small business, how many owners were asked in each market, or what the base is for each percentage.

It also does not say what "uses AI" means. One trial of a chatbot and daily use across a business would both count.

So the 84% tells you that many owners have tried it. It does not tell you that AI caused the gains they report, and it says nothing about any single country.

## The comparison to make

Government figures tell a more cautious story. Singapore's own data, which we covered earlier this month, found fewer than 4 in 100 firms with AI in their core operations. That is a different measure, which is why the two figures sit so far apart.

Meta counts owners who use AI for a task. The government counts firms that have built it into the way the work runs. Both can be true at once. Many owners have started, and few businesses have changed how they operate.

That gap is where most teams actually are, and it is a better description of your position than "late".

## What the guidance gap means for you

The finding that 86% want more guidance is a statement about support, not access. Owners have the tools. They are asking someone to show them how to use them well.

If you run a team, you are probably the someone. Nobody in the survey says the barrier is that AI is unavailable. The barrier is that nobody has shown them what good use looks like.

## What to do this month

**Pick one of the four common uses.** Writing, marketing copy, translation and customer replies are where owners start. Choose the one your team does most often and try it on a real piece of work.

**Time one task before and after.** The survey relies on opinions. You can do better with a stopwatch. Measure how long the task takes now, then again with the tool, and keep the numbers.

**Decide where the saved time goes.** The owners with gains mostly put it back into quality. Agree in advance whether you want better work, more work or growth time, so the hours do not simply disappear.

**Ask what guidance your team would want.** Cost and not knowing where to begin are the two reasons the survey gives. Ask your team which applies to them.

Meta's survey does not show that you are behind. It shows that many small firms have started on the easy tasks, and that nearly all of them would like help. The sensible move is to start where they did, measure it, and ask for guidance early.

*Source: Meta, "More than three-quarters of small business owners say AI makes them more productive, but most need more guidance", 14 October 2026, with fieldwork by YouGov. Figures are Meta's own and are self-reported by owners.*

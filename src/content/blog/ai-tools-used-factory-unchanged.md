When a department says it has adopted AI, ask what changed. A survey of Malaysian manufacturers puts two numbers from the same firms next to each other, and they point in opposite directions.

The figures come from an industry body asking its own members, not from a vendor asking its prospects. That makes them unusually easy to trust.

## What the survey found

The Federation of Malaysian Manufacturers ran the 29th edition of its Business Conditions Survey for the first half of 2026. It received 670 responses from across the country. 72% came from small and medium enterprises. The fieldwork ran from 15 July to 14 August 2026.

62% of respondents said they use AI software or productivity tools, mainly in general business operations.

Industry 4.0 implementation sits at 36%, down from 38% in the previous survey. That measures changes to the production process itself, such as connecting systems, using sensors and automating machines.

Same firms, same questionnaire. Use of AI tools is high. Change to how things get made is lower, and slipping.

## Which tools people use

Among the firms using AI tools, the survey reports ChatGPT at 83%, Gemini at 50%, Copilot at 47%, Claude at 25% and DeepSeek at 20%. The shares add up to more than 100% because firms use more than one.

Look at what the list contains. These are general-purpose assistants that a person can open in a browser on any day, usually for free or at low cost.

Nobody has to redesign a production line to use one. A manager does not need a budget, a supplier or an approval. That is why the tool figure is high and the change figure is not.

## Two separate events

It is tempting to read the 62% as the first step on a road that ends at the 36%. The survey does not say that, and the pattern does not need it to be true.

Two different things happened. Staff picked up a cheap tool on their own initiative. The organisation did not change how it works.

Only one of those costs money, and only one needs a decision from the person running the department. When someone says "we have adopted AI", they usually mean the first.

## Why the harder step is stalling

The survey gives reasons, and none of them is about AI. 69% of respondents reported higher costs. The production costs index rose to 163 from 146, while the index of general business activity fell to 90 from 103.

Asked what would move them, manufacturers named financial incentives or grants (68%), simpler approvals (40%), clearer policy direction (38%) and low-interest financing (35%).

Read that list carefully. It asks for money, speed and clarity. A firm facing rising costs and falling activity will not pay for a production change just because a chatbot is available.

## What the survey leaves out

The survey cannot tell you what the 62% do with the tools. Drafting an email and summarising a report both count. So does checking a supplier's quote or writing a first version of a safety notice.

It also cannot say how much of the use is encouraged by the firm and how much happens quietly. In many workplaces the second kind is larger than managers realise.

The figures cover Malaysian manufacturers only. Services firms, other countries and larger groups may look different, so use the survey as a prompt for your own questions and not as a forecast for your sector.

## What this means for your department

You are not running a factory, but the pattern travels. In most workplaces, individuals started using AI before anyone decided to. Your team may already be using the same tools named in the survey.

That is a start, and it is not the same as a change. Ask three questions before you describe your department as having adopted AI. Who uses a tool? Which task is done differently as a result? Which decision, process or handover changed?

If the answer to the third question is "none", you have the same picture as the survey: plenty of use and no change.

## What to do this month

**Ask your team what they already use.** A short, friendly message is enough. People rarely volunteer it, because they are unsure whether it is allowed.

**Pick one task that someone does with a tool.** Choose one with a clear before and after, such as a weekly summary that used to take an hour.

**Write down what changed in the process.** Not "Ana uses ChatGPT", but "the report now goes out on Thursday, not Friday". If nothing changed beyond one person's habit, say so.

**Decide what would move you.** The manufacturers could name what they needed. Write your own list: a budget, an approval, a policy or a colleague's time.

## A note on reading survey numbers

Be careful with the two figures. The 62% measures use of AI tools, and the 36% measures implementation of Industry 4.0 practices. They are not two readings of one thing, and the fall from 38% to 36% is not a fall in AI use.

Keep them apart in anything you write or say.

## The takeaway

High use of cheap tools and low change in how work is done is a reasonable place to be. It becomes a problem when you report the first as if it were the second.

Count the change, and keep the use as a separate line.

*Source: TNGlobal, 4 September 2026, reporting the Federation of Malaysian Manufacturers' 29th Business Conditions Survey, fielded 15 July to 14 August 2026. The survey covers Malaysian manufacturers only. The 36% and 38% figures measure Industry 4.0 implementation, not AI use.*
